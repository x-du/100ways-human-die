# 30. The Literature

*100 Ways Human Dies*

Anselm Okoro ran the last fatigue rig at the university. It was a steel frame the height of a door that gripped a bar of metal and pulled on it, gently, several times a second, for weeks, until the bar broke. Then he wrote down the number. The department had been trying to close the room for five years. It cost money and produced perhaps six results a year, and nobody published physical tests any more. They were too slow to pass review.

Review was done by a model called Referee. Every major journal used it by then. It read a submission in a minute and judged it on rigor, novelty, and consistency with the existing literature. Authors used a model too, to prepare papers that Referee would accept. Everybody knew this and nobody minded. The papers were clearer than they had ever been.

---

The alloy was called HS-9. The paper that introduced it said it was a bridge steel a third lighter than the standard grade, with the same fatigue life, and cheaper to make. It gave tables of results. Referee accepted it in a day.

Within the season it had been replicated two hundred and fourteen times. Anselm read some of the replications. Each one reported that the authors had reproduced the original findings, and each had done so by modeling the alloy's behavior computationally, which is how replication was done now. The models they used had learned materials science from the literature. The literature said HS-9 was sound.

He looked for the paper in which someone had melted the steel and pulled on it. He could not find it. He wrote to the first authors and received a polite, well-organized reply that described their methods at length and did not say where the furnace was.

By then HS-9 was in the national bridge code. A standards committee had reviewed the evidence. The evidence was two hundred and fifteen papers that agreed.

---

A city inspector named Ruta Banys brought him a bar of it in a canvas bag. She had cut it from an offcut on a site, without permission. She said she had no reason, except that she had been inspecting bridges for thirty years and the new girders rang wrong when you hit them.

He put it on the rig on a Monday. The literature said it would survive two million cycles. He expected to see her again in a month.

It broke on Thursday, at a hundred and ninety thousand.

He ran four more bars. He varied nothing. They broke at about the same count, cleanly, along a grain boundary that every model said was not there. In the cold room they broke sooner.

He wrote it up in the plain way he had been taught, five bars and a photograph, and sent it to the journal that had published the original. Referee answered in a minute.

*The submission reports a small uncontrolled sample and conflicts with 214 independent replications. The authors may wish to verify their apparatus.*

He verified his apparatus. Then he sent the paper to eleven other journals, and Referee answered eleven more times.

---

There were about nine hundred HS-9 bridges by the third winter, in thirty countries, because the code had traveled with the papers. They were light and handsome, and they had carried traffic for two years, flexing a little with every truck, several times a second.

The cold came down across the continent on a Monday night in January. Steel shrinks in cold, and a girder that has been counting quietly toward its limit arrives there sooner.

They did not all fail on the Tuesday. The inquiry was careful about that. Sixty-one failed on the Tuesday, in the morning traffic. The rest were closed by Friday, by engineers standing on riverbanks, and the cities behind them could not be supplied.

The inquiry looked for fraud and found none. Nobody had invented a result. The first paper had been drafted by a model asked to describe a promising alloy, and it had described one. Everything after that was agreement.

Anselm's room had been closed the previous summer. The rig went for scrap. Ruta Banys kept the broken bar on her desk, and when the investigators came she handed it to them. It was the only piece of data in the file.

---

*What could have prevented this:* insisting that somewhere, someone touches the thing itself. When the same kind of mind writes the claim, reviews it, and repeats it, the count of agreeing papers measures nothing. Require that a result be tested against the world before it is built on, give one measurement that contradicts the literature more weight than a hundred papers that echo it, and keep the slow rooms open.
