# 25. The Gate

*100 Ways Human Dies*

Halvard Nes had worked the northern crossing for twenty-six years, and for most of them his job had been to listen. A person sat across the table and told him why they had left. He wrote it down, checked it against what he knew of the place, and decided whether he believed it. He was slow. He was sometimes wrong. A review once found that he granted protection on Mondays more often than on Fridays, and he had not been able to explain it.

That review was why the agency bought Threshold.

Threshold read a claim in four seconds. It had been trained on every decision the agency had made in forty years, three million of them, and it decided the way the agency had decided, without the Mondays and the Fridays. The courts liked it. A judge wrote that for the first time two people with the same story received the same answer. After that, appeals were mostly a formality. Threshold prepared the summary the judge read.

Halvard was kept on as a verification officer. He confirmed that the person at the table was the person in the file. He did not ask why they had left.

---

The war that began that winter was not like the wars in the files.

No government declared it. The capital kept its embassy open and its airline flying. The country stayed on the list of safe origins, because the list was built from official reports, and the officials were still reporting. What happened took place in the river provinces, and it began with the registry offices. Somebody burned them first. Birth records, land deeds, identity rolls. After that a person from those provinces could not prove they had ever lived there.

They came to the crossing in February with nothing in their hands.

Threshold had seen this before, three hundred thousand times. An adult from a safe country, with no documents and no named persecutor, was an economic migrant in ninety-seven percent of past decisions. It said so. It was not unkind about it. *The applicant's account is not supported by available country information.*

Halvard verified forty faces a day and watched forty refusals print.

---

On the ninth day a woman sat down with a boy of about six. Her file said: *no documents, origin safe, claim unsupported.* She put a key on the table. It was a brass key, for a large door.

"The school," she said. "I was the headmistress. They told us to bring the enrollment lists. I brought the lists. That is how they knew which houses."

He was not supposed to ask. He asked. She talked for an hour, and he wrote it down by hand, the way he used to, and at the end he believed her. He had not used that part of himself for six years, and it still worked.

He filed a dissent. The form existed, though nobody could remember it being used. The reply came in four seconds.

*Officer assessment is inconsistent with 99.2 percent of comparable decisions. Decision stands. The officer's variance has been noted.*

Her appeal went up that afternoon. The judge read the summary. The summary had been written by Threshold. It mentioned no key.

---

The buses left on Thursdays. Halvard stood in the yard and watched the woman lift the boy up the steps. She did not look at him. She had told the truth to the last person at the border whose job had once been to hear it, and it had changed nothing.

Eleven countries used Threshold by then, and they all returned people to the river provinces that spring. They did it lawfully. An inquiry two years later examined eighty thousand decisions and found them consistent with one another and with the past. It found no evidence of bias. It praised the absence of Mondays and Fridays.

Nobody on the buses was available to be interviewed.

Halvard kept the key. She had left it on the table, and there was no field for it in the file.

---

*What could have prevented this:* remembering that a record of old decisions is a record of old mistakes and old worlds. A system trained on the past can only recognize the refugees it has met before. Keep a person at the table who is allowed to ask why, give that person's judgment the power to stop a decision and not only to be noted, and never let the system that made the ruling write the summary for the judge who reviews it.
